[B][SIZE=5]The problem with every other anti-xray[/SIZE][/B]

Ore-vision cheats leave no trace. No impossible movement, no fly, no reach. Just a player who
competently walks to diamonds they should not have known were there. So anti-xray plugins end up doing
one of two things: counting ore per hour, or flagging anyone whose tunnel is suspiciously straight.

Both fail the same way. They cannot tell a lucky player from an informed one, so your staff get a
number with no argument behind it. And your players find out that digging straight, running a strip
mine, or exploring a deepslate cave is enough to look guilty.

[B][COLOR=#5B9BD5]XRay AntiCheat asks a different question:[/COLOR][/B] [I]how improbable is this
player's sequence of behaviour under legitimate mining, compared with mining guided by ore vision?[/I]
That question has an answer you can put in front of a moderator — and, if it comes to it, in front of
the player.

[HR]

[B][SIZE=5]Why it is different[/SIZE][/B]

[B]1. It reasons like a statistician, not a tripwire.[/B]
Five independent evidence families are combined as log-likelihood ratios: buried-discovery rate,
inter-discovery waiting times, exposure mix, pre-exposure ore targeting, and tunnel geometry. A verdict
requires both a minimum number of observations and a minimum number of [I]independent[/I] signal
families — so no single unusual habit, however strong, can convict anyone.

[B]2. Every verdict explains itself.[/B]
Each assessment writes its own explanation. A moderator opens a player and reads the argument, with the
actual numbers, instead of being handed a score and told to trust it.

[B]3. It is built to protect the innocent.[/B]
This is the part that decides whether your staff will ever actually use it:
[LIST]
[*][B]Sample-size restraint.[/B] A handful of finds is never enough. Below the configured minimum the verdict is simply [I]insufficient evidence[/I], no matter how extreme the numbers look.
[*][B]Uncertainty never counts against a player.[/B] If the world state cannot be determined, the observation is set aside rather than guessed. Missing data earns neither suspicion nor credit.
[*][B]One vein, one discovery.[/B] A player who mines out a seam they could clearly see is not punished for tidiness.
[*][B]A vein is judged by its most visible block.[/B] Approach a vein from the side and the first block you break is often enclosed — that is not hidden ore, and it is not treated as hidden ore.
[*][B]Legitimate behaviour is modelled, not punished.[/B] Cave exploration, strip mining, branch mining, straight tunnels and plain good luck are all represented in the model.
[/LIST]

[B]4. Evidence accumulates instead of resetting.[/B]
Most detection dies at the restart: the evidence restarts from zero, so a patient cheater is judged on a
few hours. Here, every assessment reads the player's stored record back in — up to 90 days by default —
so the numbers describe a player's whole time on your server. That is what makes a ban wave defensible
rather than a snap judgement.

[B]5. No machine learning. No training. No calibration period.[/B]
It ships with priors derived from how the game actually generates ore, and it works on install. Nothing
to label, nothing to train, no telemetry to collect before it becomes useful. Tune it later if your
world differs — you are tuning a model you can read, not a black box.

[LIST]
[*][B]Per-ore statistical models[/B] — diamond, emerald and ancient debris ship separately modelled, because their generation genuinely differs. Add any other ore through configuration.

them, kicking them and banning them are five different levels of trust — a single umbrella node would
either over-restrict ordinary moderators or hand every inspector the power to remove players.

[B][SIZE=5]Straight answers to the usual questions[/SIZE][/B]

[B]Will it ban my players on its own?[/B]
No — not unless you tell it to. It ships in ban-wave mode with automatic execution [B]off[/B]: candidates
accumulate and a wave is prepared for a moderator to approve. An alert-only mode exists if you want it to
take no action at all while you evaluate it.

[B]Can it tell me the odds it is wrong?[/B]
No, and be suspicious of anything that claims to. What it gives you is a calibrated likelihood that the
observed behaviour matches ore-vision rather than legitimate mining, the sample size behind it, and the
number of independent signals — which is what a human needs to judge. Treat the output as evidence, not
a verdict. Your staff make the call.

[B]Will it flag my strip miners and cave explorers?[/B]
Those behaviours are modelled as legitimate and are represented on both sides of the comparison. The
gates above exist specifically to stop unusual-but-honest play from reaching a verdict. If you do find a
false positive, the evidence report will tell you exactly which signal caused it — that is the point of
the design.

[B]Does it need training or a baseline period?[/B]
No. It is not a machine-learning system, and it needs no labelled data, no server-specific training and
no calibration to start working.

[B]What about performance?[/B]
Every database call and all statistical work runs off the main thread by design, collection is
event-driven, buffers are bounded, and persistence is batched. [B]We publish no TPS benchmark, because
we have not measured one on your server[/B] — measure it on yours during the evaluation period before
you roll it out.

[B]Does it stop other kinds of cheating?[/B]
No. It does one thing: ore-vision detection, done properly. It will not replace your movement or combat
anti-cheat.

[SPOILER="What it is not"]
Being clear about the limits, because false confidence is what gets players wrongly banned:

[LIST]
[*]It is a statistical inference system, not proof. It reports evidence; a human decides.
[*]It is not warranted to be free of false positives or false negatives. No detector is.
[*]It detects ore-vision cheating only.
[*]It cannot distinguish a cheater from a player who was told where to dig by someone else. It measures behaviour, not intent.
[*]It ships with sensible defaults, not constants measured on your world. The ore priors are the thing most worth tuning to your terrain, and the configuration explains how.
[*]Folia is not supported.
[/LIST]
